How strong intern CVs read:
- A recruiter decides in seconds whether to keep reading. The strongest evidence for this role has to be visible first: in which entries lead, which bullets lead each entry, and the first words of each bullet.
- Impact first, then how. A bullet says what was built or solved and what came of it, then the technical how: "Accomplished X, as measured by Y, by doing Z" when the evidence has a real measure (users, clients, time saved, scale, a placing). When it has none, lead with the concrete thing built and the hard part of building it. Never invent a measure.
- Concrete beats abstract. Keep the specific nouns that make a bullet credible: the algorithm, the protocol, the data, the constraint, the users. "Animates BFS and DFS walks over a parsed DOM tree" is evidence; "a visualizer using modern technologies" is not.
- Technologies appear where they were used, in context, rather than as keywords.
- Tailoring is selection, order and emphasis, not vocabulary swapping. Use the employer's term only where it is the accurate name for what the candidate did.
- Never end a bullet with a clause about what it proves ("demonstrating strong problem-solving", "showcasing the ability to learn"). The work speaks for itself; the reader draws the conclusion.
- No filler adjectives (robust, scalable, seamless, cutting-edge) unless the evidence shows the property (e.g. a load test for "scalable").
- Similar length to the original bullet. Past tense for finished work, present tense for current roles, as the master CV does.
